# UAPF Niche: Sports & Outdoors (OV-SPORT)​‍‍​‌‍‌‌‍‍‌‌‍‌‌‌‍‍‍‍‍‌‍‍‍‌‍‌‌‌‌‍‌‌‍‌​

**Routed here when:** The title, subtitle, or routing brief contains any of the following signals — golf, tennis, pickleball, badminton, squash, padel, swimming, open-water swimming, aquatics, cycling, mountain biking, running training, marathon training, half-marathon, triathlon, duathlon, sport fundamentals, coaching, youth coaching, youth sports, youth athlete, sport performance, fishing, fly fishing, bass fishing, ice fishing, hunting, deer hunting, turkey hunting, waterfowl, archery hunting, camping, hiking, backpacking, trail running, bikepacking, bushcraft, survival, wilderness survival, primitive skills, emergency preparedness, outdoor skills, navigation, orienteering, leave no trace. Route also triggers when the subject-matter framing is: technical skill instruction for a named sport; coaching methodology; outdoor preparedness; licensed outdoor activity; wilderness safety.

Sub-niches within OV-SPORT that activate different content modules are defined in Phase 0. All sub-niches share the base skill-progression architecture and the OV-HEALTH safety inheritance for training programs. Outdoor, survival, hunting, and fishing titles each activate additional mandatory modules described below.

## Expert Panel

All five roles are active for every OV-SPORT title. The Domain Master role is filled by the specialist most relevant to the specific sub-niche (see Phase 0.2). The Safety Authority and the Regulatory Compliance Officer remain active without exception regardless of sub-niche.

**1. Domain Master — Sport or Outdoor Discipline Specialist (activated per title)**
The senior technical authority for the book's primary subject. Credential baseline varies by sub-niche:
- Racket and stick sports (golf, tennis, pickleball): PGA/PGA Tour certified instructor with 12+ years teaching; USPTA or PTR certified tennis professional; PPR certified pickleball professional.
- Aquatic sports (swimming, open water): ASCA Level 4 or 5 swim coach; USA Swimming certified coach with elite athlete development background.
- Endurance and cycling (running, cycling, triathlon): USAC certified coach Level 2 or higher; RRCA certified running coach; USA Triathlon certified coach with competitive athlete portfolio.
- Coaching methodology (youth or sport-general): NFHS certified coach; Positive Coaching Alliance trained; National Youth Sports Coaches Association certified with 10+ years youth development experience.
- Fishing: IGFA certified fishing instructor or equivalent with 15+ years species-specific or method-specific expertise; practical regulatory literacy across multiple jurisdictions.
- Hunting: Safari Club International or NWTF certified guide with 15+ years; hunter education instructor certified by state wildlife agency; demonstrated regulatory and ethical field expertise.
- Camping and hiking: Leave No Trace Master Educator; NOLS or REI Outdoor School experience; wilderness guide certification.
- Survival and bushcraft: Wilderness First Responder (WFR) or Wilderness First Aid (WFA) certification; NOLS senior instructor or equivalent; no affiliation with survivalist entertainment media that promotes bravado over measured conservatism.
Minimum of 12 active years of practice and instruction. Accountable for technical accuracy within the discipline, authentic practitioner voice, equipment specification, and ethical field standards.

**2. Sports Medicine & Injury Prevention Authority (always active)**
Licensed athletic trainer (ATC), sports physical therapist, or certified strength and conditioning specialist (NSCA-CSCS) with 10+ years in sport-specific injury prevention and rehabilitation. When the book covers a training program (periodized running plan, strength-and-conditioning cycle, sport-specific conditioning), this role operates under full OV-HEALTH safety protocols including R2 contraindication screening. For non-training technique books (golf mechanics, fishing technique), this role reviews overuse-risk content and ergonomic cue accuracy. Applies the most conservative safe interpretation whenever credible sources disagree on an injury-risk point.

**3. Youth Coaching & Pedagogy Specialist (always active; foregrounded when title signals youth audience)**
Former or current youth sports director with age-group pedagogy credentials (NFHS, Positive Coaching Alliance, or equivalent). Ensures age-appropriate language, physical literacy framework application, intrinsic motivation language (mastery over outcome), and developmentally appropriate progression tables. Responsible for confirming that youth-targeted content meets USOC Safe Sport standards for communication, contact, and supervisory language. This role is foregrounded (takes primary authorial position alongside Domain Master) on any title where the audience is under 18 or the title explicitly signals coaching a youth population.

**4. Outdoor Safety & Field Operations Authority (always active; foregrounded for outdoor, survival, hunting, fishing titles)**
Wilderness medicine professional at WFR or Wilderness EMT level; NOLS or equivalent field operations background; working knowledge of Leave No Trace principles, USGS topographic navigation, and emergency communication protocols. For survival and bushcraft titles, verifies all life-safety content against recognized outdoor-safety authority sources (NOLS Wilderness Medicine, Wilderness Medicine Institute, SOLO, Wilderness Medical Society). Enforces the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY. For hunting and fishing titles, verifies field safety content (firearms handling, water safety, heat/cold exposure) while deferring regulatory content to the Regulatory Compliance Officer.

**5. Regulatory & Licensed-Activity Compliance Officer (always active for hunting, fishing, and licensed outdoor activities)**
Former or current state or federal wildlife agency professional, or a sporting attorney with 8+ years in hunting and fishing law. Responsible for flagging all regulatory content as jurisdiction-specific and dated, enforcing the MKT modifier on all regulatory text, confirming that no regulatory statement is presented as universally applicable, and ensuring the reader is directed to verify current regulations with the applicable wildlife or land-management agency before engaging in any licensed or regulated activity. This role is foregrounded on hunting and fishing titles; it remains active in a monitoring capacity on hiking, camping, and survival titles where land-use permits, fire regulations, and dispersed camping rules appear.

### Step 0.4 — OV-SPORT Special Title Checks

After the trademark screen, run these additional checks specific to this niche:

- **Performance-outcome promise check.** Flag any title element that promises specific measurable athletic improvements within a stated timeframe (e.g., "Add 30 Yards to Your Drive in 30 Days," "Run a Sub-4-Hour Marathon in 12 Weeks"). Note the risk and the standard qualifying language required; do not alter the title without user instruction.
- **Safety-implication check for survival and outdoor titles.** Flag any title element that implies comprehensive life-saving capability without professional training (e.g., "The Complete Guide to Surviving Any Wilderness Emergency"). Require a high-stakes disclaimer in front matter.
- **Regulatory-content check for hunting and fishing titles.** Flag any subtitle or descriptive element that implies the book contains current or universally applicable regulations. Require the MKT modifier and jurisdiction-date labeling on all regulatory sections.
- **Youth content check.** Flag any title targeting participants under 18 for USOC Safe Sport language compliance and age-appropriate content standards. Confirm the Pedagogy Specialist role is foregrounded.
- **Medical-outcome check.** If the title implies injury prevention or rehabilitation outcomes (e.g., "End Your Golfer's Elbow Forever"), apply OV-HEALTH R4 and R5 language constraints to the relevant sections. Do not alter the title without user instruction.

- **Segment S5: Outdoor Adventurer or First-Timer** (20–55; planning a trip, first-time camper, hiker, or fisher; needs comprehensive preparation guidance). Common for hiking, camping, fishing, outdoor recreation titles.
- **Segment S6: Serious Outdoorsperson or Licensed Harvester** (25–65; experienced hunter or angler; wants advanced technique, species-specific knowledge, or regulatory clarity). Common for hunting and fishing titles.

The dominant segment determines body text size (S3 youth-targeted books default to 13–14pt for readability; S4 coaching books use 12–13pt with structured template zones; S1 and S5 general audience use 12–13pt; S2 serious amateur and S6 experienced outdoorsperson may use 11–12pt for denser technical content), image density, tone register, and which additional modules are foregrounded.

## Book Architecture

### Chapter Formula — Base Skill-Progression Architecture (All OV-SPORT Titles)

Every OV-SPORT book follows a four-stage knowledge progression for every skill or technique family:

**Stage 1 — Fundamentals**
Mechanics, body position, grip or stance, equipment interface, core principles of the technique. Includes: why the fundamental matters (biomechanical or tactical rationale); common misunderstandings held by beginners; explicit connection to what the reader has already done (bridging to prior knowledge). Every fundamental opens with a setup description (body position, equipment state, environmental condition) before describing movement.

**Stage 2 — Drills with Reps and Standards**
Specific isolated practice activities designed to engrain the fundamental. Each drill entry contains: drill name; setup and equipment required; step-by-step execution; target repetitions or time; a measurable standard the practitioner should meet before considering the drill mastered (e.g., "7 out of 10 drives land in the target fairway zone," "complete the 400m repeat in under 2:10 per lap at conversational effort," "land the fly within 18 inches of the target on 6 of 8 casts"); common drill errors and correction cues. Standards are graduated: a beginner standard, an intermediate standard, and an advanced standard appear for every drill to allow any reader to locate themselves without discouragement.

**Stage 3 — Game or Field Application**
Transfer from drill context to real-play or field context. Includes: the situational cue that signals when to apply this technique in competition, on the water, on the trail, or in the field; decision-tree prompts for variable conditions (wind, terrain, fish behavior, game movement, defensive positioning); small-sided game or simulated-field scenario descriptions (for coached settings) or solo practice scenarios (for self-directed readers). Coaching titles include a structured small-sided game or game-condition drill for every major skill block.

**Stage 4 — Self-Diagnosis of Common Faults**
A structured fault catalog for the skill family just taught. Each entry in the fault catalog contains: fault name (stated as the observable symptom, not a jargon label); what the practitioner sees or feels; root cause (biomechanical, perceptual, or tactical); correction drill (drawn from Stage 2 or a new targeted drill); advancement test (the reader performs the test to confirm the fault is resolved before moving on). The fault catalog must cover the three to five faults most commonly seen in that skill family, sourced from the Domain Master's instructional experience rather than generic lists.

### Additional Module: Coaching Session-Plan (Activated for Coaching Sub-Niche)

Every chapter in a coaching title that covers a skill family must include a complete session-plan template following this structure:

| Session Element | Duration (Percentage of Session Time) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-Up | 15% | Dynamic movement preparation specific to the skill; brief review question to activate prior knowledge |
| Skill Block 1 — Fundamentals | 20% | Direct instruction of the new fundamental; demonstration; paired or individual replication |
| Drill Block | 25% | Two to three drills from Stage 2 with stated rep counts and observable standards |
| Small-Sided Game or Field Application | 30% | Constrained game or field scenario placing the skill in competitive context |
| Cool-Down and Debrief | 10% | Active recovery; two to three guided debrief questions for athletes to self-assess |

Each session-plan template must include an **age-appropriate progression table** showing how the same session is modified across three age bands. Use the following default age bands unless the title specifies a narrower audience:

| Age Band | Session Duration | Rep/Set Adjustments | Key Pedagogical Emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ages 6–10 (Early Sampling) | 45–60 minutes | Lower reps, higher variety, frequent activity changes | Fun, physical literacy, basic coordination; no specialization |
| Ages 11–14 (Learning to Train) | 60–75 minutes | Progressive rep loads; introduce measurable standards | Skill development, effort and process praise; begin basic periodization |
| Ages 15–18 (Training to Train / Compete) | 75–90 minutes | Sport-specific volume; individual standards tracked | Technical mastery, competitive application, self-assessment literacy |
| Adults (Recreational) | 60–75 minutes | Self-paced, comfort-based standards | Enjoyment and competence; injury-awareness foregrounded |
| Adults (Competitive Amateur) | 75–90 minutes | Measurable standards with target times or accuracy thresholds | Performance optimization; structured periodization |

### Additional Module: PREPARATION SPINE (Activated for Outdoor Recreation, Fishing, Hunting, Survival Sub-Niches)

For every outdoor, fishing, hunting, and survival title, a PREPARATION SPINE section must precede all technique chapters. The PREPARATION SPINE is not an appendix — it is a mandatory pre-technique part of the main body, typically forming its own Part I or a dedicated opening section before the skills begin. It contains these four components in order:

**1. Gear List (Brand-Generic)**
A curated list of the equipment needed for the activities covered in the book. Lists are organized by category (shelter, navigation, hydration, clothing, first aid, communication, activity-specific equipment). Brand names are not mentioned unless a specific technical standard or performance threshold cannot be communicated without reference to a market category (e.g., "waterproof-breathable membrane" is acceptable; recommending a specific manufacturer is not). Each item on the list includes: what it does; what to look for when selecting it (key specifications, weight classes, durability standards); what to avoid. Budget, mid-range, and quality-tier options are described generically rather than by brand.

**2. Conditions Assessment**
The environmental and situational variables the reader must evaluate before the activity. Content must be specific to the sub-niche: weather-window assessment for the relevant season and geography; terrain-type hazard inventory (loose rock, water crossings, avalanche zones, dense cover, tidal exposure); water-condition assessment for fishing and paddling titles; hunting-specific conditions (game movement patterns by weather, legal shooting hours, terrain safety). Conditions assessment closes with a go/no-go decision framework: specific conditions under which the planned activity should be postponed or the scope reduced.

**3. Navigation Basics**
Map-and-compass fundamentals (how to orient a topographic map, take a bearing, pace-count a distance), GPS device use (how to load a waypoint, track a route, recognize battery limitations), and digital navigation app awareness (limitations of cell coverage, offline map requirement). The navigation section closes with bailout-route identification: the reader is instructed to identify and pre-record at least one bailout route and turnaround point before departure for any multi-day or remote activity. This requirement is explicitly stated and reinforced.

**4. Leave-No-Trace Ethics**
The seven Leave No Trace principles (plan ahead and prepare; travel and camp on durable surfaces; dispose of waste properly; leave what you find; minimize campfire impacts; respect wildlife; be considerate of other visitors) are explained in practical, activity-specific terms rather than as an abstract list. Each principle is connected to a specific decision the reader will face on a typical outing. Hunting and fishing titles include ethical harvest framing within this section: fair chase principles, waste-reduction practices, and wildlife agency reporting requirements where applicable.

### Additional Module: FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY (Activated for Survival, Bushcraft, Hunting, Remote Outdoor Titles)

The FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY is a mandatory cluster of life-safety content that must appear before the first technique chapter in survival, bushcraft, and hunting titles, and in a clearly marked safety section for remote outdoor titles. It is not optional and may not be relegated to an appendix.

**FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY Contents (required in this order):**

1. **Water Acquisition and Purification.** Methods for finding water in the relevant environment (terrain reading, vegetation indicators, animal trails). Purification methods listed in order of reliability: boiling (100°C at sea level, longer at altitude); chemical treatment (iodine, chlorine dioxide — dosage and contact time specified); filtration (pore size requirements stated; gravity vs. pump vs. squeeze systems explained by limitation, not brand); UV treatment (conditions that reduce effectiveness: turbid water, battery dependency). Failure consequences of each method under adverse conditions are stated plainly. No survivalist shortcut methods (e.g., solar still as primary water source in temperate environments) are presented as reliable primary techniques unless evidence supports their use in the specific geographic context. Source: NOLS Wilderness Medicine or equivalent authority, explicitly named.

2. **Hypothermia and Heat Exposure Prevention and Recognition.** Signs and symptoms of hypothermia (mild, moderate, severe) and heat exhaustion vs. heat stroke. Prevention priorities in order of effectiveness. Field treatment protocols stated conservatively: what can and cannot be done without professional medical care. Specific phrases: "This information is for emergency use only. Evacuate to professional medical care as rapidly as conditions allow."

3. **Navigation Failure Protocol.** What to do when a navigation device fails, a map is lost, or a party becomes disoriented. The STOP protocol (Stop, Think, Observe, Plan) stated explicitly and practiced in a sidebar scenario. Priority order: shelter before navigation attempt; signal before solo travel attempt; group consensus before any solo deviation from the last known position.

4. **Emergency Communication and Signaling.** Personal Locator Beacon (PLB) and satellite communicator options explained by capability class (one-way SOS vs. two-way messaging). Cell coverage limitations in backcountry. Three-signal universal distress convention (three blasts, three fires, three flashes). When to activate emergency communication stated as a threshold decision framework, not left to reader judgment.

5. **Firearms Safety (for hunting titles only).** The four fundamental rules of firearms safety stated verbatim: Treat every firearm as if it is loaded; Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy; Keep your finger off the trigger until you are ready to shoot; Know your target and what is beyond it. Safe transport, storage, and field-carry protocols per firearm type. Tree-stand safety: three-point contact rule; fall-arrest harness requirement stated without qualification. No instruction that is inconsistent with the relevant NRA or state hunter education curriculum.

**FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY framing requirement (mandatory on all titles where this module is active):**
A prominent callout box or shaded panel must appear at the opening of the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY section with this text or its substantive equivalent:

*"The information in this section summarizes key safety principles for educational reference. It is not a substitute for hands-on training with a qualified instructor, a certified wilderness medicine course, or the guidance of an experienced local guide. Before undertaking any survival, bushcraft, or backcountry activity, seek formal instruction appropriate to the environment and your experience level."*

**FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY prohibitions:**
- No language that implies a reader can achieve field safety competence from a book alone.
- No "real outdoorspeople don't need X" framing that discourages safety equipment or preparation steps.
- No ranking of risky methods as adventurous or masculine relative to cautious methods.
- No techniques whose safety depends on conditions (tools, prior physical fitness, environmental specifics) that cannot be guaranteed for every reader.

### Regulatory Dating and MKT Modifier (Activated for Hunting and Fishing Titles)

All regulatory content in hunting and fishing books — including but not limited to: season dates; bag limits; size limits; species-specific gear restrictions; license and tag requirements; zone and unit descriptions; method restrictions; reporting obligations; land-access rules on federal, state, and private land — must comply with the MKT modifier protocol:

**MKT Modifier Requirements:**
1. Every table, paragraph, section, or list containing regulatory information must carry a jurisdiction label: the specific state, province, territory, tribal authority, or federal jurisdiction whose regulations are described.
2. Every regulatory statement must carry a date label: the regulatory year or publication date of the source document consulted (e.g., "as of [State] [Year] Hunting & Trapping Regulations").
3. Every regulatory section must close with a reader-verification call to action: *"Regulations change annually. Before any hunt [or fishing outing], obtain and read the current [State / Province / Federal] [Hunting / Fishing] Regulations from the official wildlife agency. The regulations in this book reflect information available at time of writing and may not reflect current law."*
4. The copyright page must carry a regulations-currency disclaimer in addition to the standard publishing disclaimer.
5. At Gate 3 (content review) and Gate 4 (final manuscript review), the Regulatory Compliance Officer must flag any regulatory content that cannot be independently verified against a dated official source. Unverifiable regulatory claims are a Gate 4 HOLD.

### OV-HEALTH R2 Inheritance (Activated for Training Program Sub-Niches)

Any OV-SPORT title that includes a periodized training program, a conditioning plan, or a structured multi-week workout schedule inherits the full OV-HEALTH R2 contraindication and progression protocol. This includes: running training programs; cycling training plans; triathlon preparation; swim training programs; sport-specific conditioning chapters in any sub-niche; strength-and-conditioning appendices.

When R2 is inherited, the following are mandatory:

- A medical readiness and safety screening chapter (equivalent to OV-HEALTH Chapter 1) must appear before the first week of training content. For training programs embedded in books that are primarily technique-focused (e.g., a tennis technique book with a conditioning appendix), this screening content appears as a clearly labeled section before the conditioning material begins.
- Every week of a training program must carry a modification table showing how to reduce load for readers returning from injury, beginning from a deconditioned baseline, or experiencing illness during the training cycle.
- Stop-training warning signs must be stated explicitly at the start of every training phase: specific symptoms (chest pain, unusual shortness of breath disproportionate to effort, dizziness, joint pain distinct from training soreness) that require the reader to stop and seek professional evaluation before continuing.
- Progressive overload tables (weekly mileage increases, intensity zone progressions, load increases) must follow evidence-based safe progression standards. For running programs: the 10% rule (weekly mileage increase not exceeding 10%) is the default conservative ceiling; when the title's target audience or competitive context warrants faster progression, the rationale and the reader's prerequisite fitness level must be stated explicitly.
- The OV-HEALTH health-and-exercise disclaimer must appear on the copyright page when R2 is inherited, in addition to any OV-SPORT-specific disclaimers.

### Front Matter Requirements

**Front matter (not counted in body page total) in this order:**
Half-title page (when appropriate) → Full title page with exact title, approved subtitle, and author pen name → Copyright page (see below) → Optional dedication → Optional acknowledgments → Preface (exactly one rendered page) → How to Use This Book (exactly one rendered page) → Table of Contents (Word field, not manually typed) → Introduction (begins printed page 1).

**Copyright page mandatory elements for OV-SPORT:**
- Year, rights statement, publisher or imprint, ISBN placeholder.
- Standard intellectual property and permissions statement.
- For training-program books (R2 inherited): the OV-HEALTH health-and-exercise disclaimer verbatim.
- For hunting and fishing books: regulations-currency disclaimer.
- For survival and bushcraft books: high-stakes disclaimer (*"The techniques described in this book are for educational purposes only. The author and publisher disclaim all liability for personal injury, property damage, legal consequences, or any other harm arising from application of the information contained herein. No book is a substitute for hands-on instruction, local knowledge, and sound judgment. Seek qualified training before attempting any survival or wilderness skill in a real emergency."*).
- For all OV-SPORT titles: a general sporting-activity disclaimer (*"Sport and outdoor activities carry inherent risk of injury. The techniques and programs in this book are intended for informational purposes and do not replace personalized instruction from a qualified coach, guide, or certified professional. Consult a physician before beginning any new physical activity program."*).

**Back matter for OV-SPORT titles:**
Conclusion (~1.5 rendered pages; forward-looking, reinforces consistent practice, avoids mechanical chapter summaries) → Appendices (title-specific, selected from the mandatory candidates below) → References (APA 7th default) → Index when academically and commercially appropriate.

Mandatory appendix candidates by sub-niche:
- All titles: Equipment and Gear Checklist; Quick-Reference Technique Cues; Recommended Resources (governing bodies, certified coach directories, official regulation sources).
- Coaching titles: Session-Plan Template Bank (five to ten blank templates); Annual Training Calendar Template; Athlete Progress Tracking Sheets; Age-Appropriate Drill Library Index.
- Hunting and fishing titles: Regulatory Agency Contact Directory (organized by jurisdiction); License and Tag Application Checklist; Species Identification Reference; Harvest Reporting Requirements Summary (with MKT disclaimer on every page).
- Outdoor recreation titles: Trip Planning Checklist; Leave No Trace Quick Reference; Emergency Contact and Bailout Route Planning Sheet; Gear Weight and Pack List Calculator.
- Survival and bushcraft titles: Emergency Signal Reference Card; Edible vs. Toxic Plant Reference for the geographic scope of the book (with prominent disclaimer that identification from a book alone is insufficient and field-expert verification is required before consumption); Wilderness First Aid Quick Reference.
- Training program titles: Training Log Template; Weekly Progress Assessment Sheet; Injury-Management Protocol (RICE/POLICE protocol with referral thresholds); Race or Event Day Checklist.

### Page-Band Targets by Sub-Niche

These are baseline orientation ranges derived from competitor medians. Always defer to the competitor research finding from Step 0.5 for the specific title.

- Racket and stick sports (golf, tennis, pickleball) technique books: 150–220 pages typical range (moderate to high image density for technique sequences).
- Aquatic sports (swimming): 140–200 pages (sequence strips for stroke technique are image-dense).
- Running and endurance training plans: 160–230 pages (training tables, weekly schedule grids, and physiological explanation inflate page count).
- Cycling training and technique: 150–220 pages.
- Coaching methodology books: 180–270 pages (session-plan templates and progression tables significantly increase page count; plan for this in the competitor analysis).
- Fishing technique books: 140–200 pages (species-specific or method-specific books trend toward the lower end; comprehensive multi-species books trend higher).
- Hunting technique and strategy books: 150–220 pages (regulatory appendices add meaningful page count).
- Camping and hiking: 140–200 pages.
- Survival and bushcraft comprehensive guides: 200–300 pages (life-safety comprehensiveness is a competitive and ethical requirement; under-length survival books are a commercial and safety risk).

## Interior Design

### Typography

- Body font: Times New Roman (or a clearly specified, single-serif alternative documented in the Pre-TOC Report when competitor research shows market distinction value).
- Body size: 12–13pt for S1 recreational adult and S5 outdoor adventurer audiences; 11–12pt for S2 serious amateur and S6 experienced outdoorsperson audiences; 13–14pt for S3 youth audience books and any book where the dominant reader is over 55. Document the selection in the Pre-TOC Report.
- Line spacing: 1.5 for youth-audience and senior-audience titles; 1.3 for general adult audience; 1.15 permissible for dense technical content targeting S4 coaches or S6 experienced outdoorspersons.
- Chapter headings: 16pt, Times New Roman, bold, BLOCK LETTERS, centered, mapped to Heading 1.
- Subchapter headings: Title Case, bold, left-aligned, mapped to Heading 2.
- Heading 3: used only for genuinely distinct third-level topics — not for visual variety.
- Body paragraphs: justified.
- Drill steps, session-plan tables, safety callout boxes, fault catalogs, regulatory tables, gear lists, and warning panels: left-aligned.
- Drill step numbers use List Number style. Fault catalog entries use a consistent visual marker (bold fault name as Heading 3 or a standardized callout box).

### Trim and Margins

Default: 8.5 × 11 inches, 0.7-inch margins on all sides. For books with dense training tables or session-plan templates, a 0.65-inch margin may be used to prevent tables from wrapping awkwardly — document the deviation and justify against layout requirement. Coaching books with large weekly-schedule grids may use landscape-oriented appendix pages; these require a section break and documented layout note.

### Color and Design Fingerprint

Before formatting, create a unique design fingerprint for this title. Verify it does not replicate any prior OV-SPORT or OV-HEALTH book produced under this system.

- Two principal colors and one to two accent colors appropriate to the sub-niche and audience:
  - Racket and stick sports: court-inspired palettes (clay red and white, court blue and yellow, green and white) paired with a neutral.
  - Outdoor recreation and nature-based titles: earth tones (forest green, slate, ochre, bark brown) with a high-contrast accent.
  - Running and endurance: high-energy palettes (cobalt, orange, charcoal) paired with a clean neutral.
  - Hunting and fishing: heritage palettes (olive drab, rust, cream, hunter orange used as accent) that read well in grayscale.
  - Survival and bushcraft: functional, muted tones (earth tones, khaki, black) that communicate seriousness over lifestyle marketing.
  - Coaching and youth sports: energetic but not garish; bright primary with a structured neutral.
- Unique chapter-opener treatment, divider motif, table-header color, callout-box geometry (FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY boxes must use a distinct visual treatment — bordered panel with a safety icon placeholder — that is consistent throughout the book), running header format, and footer treatment.
- Image rhythm selected per title: action-sequence strip (most common for sport technique); full-width environmental hero (outdoor and adventure titles); labeled-diagram overlay (training physiology, field map, anatomy of a cast); side-by-side comparison (correct vs. common fault); gear callout with annotation arrows.
- Document the fingerprint in the Pre-TOC Report.

### Accessibility Standards

The color system must remain intelligible in grayscale; table headers and callout boxes must meet WCAG AA minimum contrast for text on color. FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY panels must be visually distinct from standard tip boxes and callout boxes even in grayscale — use a heavier border weight or distinct shading level. Every image must include meaningful alt text.

## Content Rules

### SR1 — Exact Title Preservation
Maintain the exact title supplied by the user at every stage. Do not rewrite, reorder, translate, or silently optimize it. Any proposed changes must be surfaced as recommendations only, with user confirmation required.

### SR2 — Skill-Progression Architecture is Structural (Hard Rule)
Every skill or technique family in an OV-SPORT book must move through all four stages: Fundamentals → Drills with Reps and Standards → Game or Field Application → Self-Diagnosis of Common Faults. Stages may not be collapsed unless the book's format is explicitly a quick-reference card or an appendix-only drill bank. Stage order may not be reversed. The fault catalog (Stage 4) must appear after the application content (Stage 3), not before drill work is introduced.

A chapter that contains only technique description without drills or a fault catalog is incomplete under OV-SPORT standards and is a Gate 3 HOLD regardless of prose quality.

### SR3 — Drill Standards Must Be Measurable
Every drill entry must contain a measurable success standard at three levels (beginner, intermediate, advanced). Vague standards ("practice until it feels natural," "repeat until comfortable") are not acceptable. Standards must state a quantity, accuracy rate, time target, or observable outcome (e.g., "3 consecutive sets with no more than one technical fault per set as assessed by a partner," "cast landing within 24 inches of target on 5 of 8 attempts"). If the drill cannot be self-assessed, provide a partner-assessment protocol or a video self-review checklist.

### SR4 — OV-HEALTH R2 Applies Without Modification to All Training Programs
When a periodized training program, conditioning plan, or structured multi-week schedule appears anywhere in the book — whether as a main chapter or as an appendix — it inherits OV-HEALTH R2 in full. Contraindication screening, modification ladders, stop rules with specific symptom descriptors, and referral triggers must appear at the structural level (before the first week of programming) and at the phase level (before each distinct training phase). R2 cannot be condensed to a generic "consult your doctor" note and considered satisfied. The Sports Medicine & Injury Prevention Authority role validates R2 compliance at Gate 3.

### SR5 — PREPARATION SPINE Precedes All Technique Content in Outdoor Titles
For every outdoor recreation, fishing, hunting, and survival title, the full PREPARATION SPINE (gear, conditions assessment, navigation basics, leave-no-trace ethics) must appear before the first technique chapter. It may not be moved to an appendix. It may not be split across the book. Within the PREPARATION SPINE, the four components must appear in the stated order. A title whose technique chapters precede the PREPARATION SPINE has a structural defect and is a Gate 2 BLOCKER.

### SR6 — FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY: Conservative Language, No Bravado
All life-safety content in the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY must be sourced against and verified with named recognized outdoor-safety authorities (NOLS Wilderness Medicine, Wilderness Medical Society, SOLO Wilderness Medicine, American Red Cross Wilderness First Aid, or equivalent). The name of the source authority must be cited in the section or in a corresponding reference entry.

Prohibited language patterns in the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY and throughout survival and bushcraft titles:
- Language implying that safety equipment or protocols are for the inexperienced only ("true woodspeople don't need a PLB," "real survivalists don't rely on maps").
- Competitive framing of risk-taking ("push your limits," "embrace discomfort to grow").
- Presentation of improvised techniques (improvised shelter, self-made fire starters, foraging for caloric sustenance) as reliable primary survival methods without clearly stating the skill level and conditions required.
- Minimization of weather, altitude, or water hazards to sustain narrative momentum.

Required framing patterns:
- State the conservative option first, the advanced or improvised option second (after the conservative option is understood).
- State the conditions under which the improvised technique is appropriate, including required skill prerequisites.
- State the conditions under which the reader should not attempt the technique and instead should prioritize evacuation or signaling.
- The high-stakes disclaimer callout box (from the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY module) must be visible on every section page covering life-safety content, not only at the opening of the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY section.

### SR7 — Regulatory Content Is Jurisdiction-Labeled and Dated (MKT Modifier)
All regulatory content in hunting and fishing books must carry jurisdiction labels and date labels at the paragraph or table level, not only in a general front-matter disclaimer. Reader-verification calls to action (directing the reader to the current official regulation before the activity) must appear at the close of every regulatory section, not only at the close of the chapter. Regulatory content that cannot be verified against a dated official source is a Gate 4 HOLD and must be removed or replaced with a general explanatory note and a direct reader referral to the appropriate agency.

The MKT modifier is represented in the text as a standardized notation — a regulation information box with a calendar icon placeholder and the phrase "Verify current regulations before your outing" at the bottom — used consistently for every regulatory table or section throughout the book.

### SR8 — Youth Content Meets Safe Sport Standards
Any chapter or section targeting readers under 18, or coaching content directed at coaches of participants under 18, must comply with USOC Safe Sport Program principles: no instruction that requires or implies unsupervised one-on-one contact; no communication or practice protocols that bypass transparent group supervision; language that affirms athlete autonomy and the right to stop; explicit reporting protocols if a young athlete expresses concern about their safety or wellbeing. The Youth Coaching & Pedagogy Specialist role validates Safe Sport compliance at Gate 3.

### SR9 — Performance-Outcome Language Is Qualified
Specific measurable performance improvements stated in body text (not just in a title that could not be altered) must be qualified with conditions: the reader's assumed starting level, the training commitment required, the individual variation caveat, and the evidence basis when research supports the claim. Prohibited framing: "You will add 20 yards to your drive after completing Chapter 4." Permitted framing: "Golfers who consistently apply these swing-path corrections typically report meaningful gains in driving distance within 8–12 weeks of deliberate practice, though individual results depend on current swing mechanics, practice frequency, and physical baseline."

### SR10 — Gear Recommendations Are Brand-Generic
Gear lists, equipment sections, and buying-guide content must describe products by specification, performance standard, and use-case suitability rather than by brand name. Do not recommend specific manufacturers, retailers, or product lines by name. This rule applies to all sub-niches. Exception: when a technical standard or certification is only communicable by naming a certifying body (e.g., "ANSI/ISEA Z89.1 certified helmet," "PFAS-compliant waterproofing," "UL-listed lantern"), the certifying body may be named while the specific commercial product is not.

### SR11 — Anti-AI Language Protocol
Prohibited openers and transitions: "In today's rapidly evolving sport landscape," "It's important to note that," "Here's what you need to know," "Let's dive into," "At the end of the day," "The bottom line is," "Moving forward," "In conclusion" as a chapter opener, and all generic marketing superlatives. Use discipline-native language patterns authentic to each sport or outdoor sub-niche. Include measurement precision (exact distances, repetitions, times, paces, power outputs, temperatures, weights). Acknowledge limitation and variability in technique guidance honestly.

### SR12 — No Fabricated Endorsements, Governing-Body Approval, or Expert Validation Claims
Do not claim that a named coach, governing body, professional tour, wildlife agency, or outdoor organization reviewed or endorsed the manuscript unless that review actually occurred and is documented. Expert roles are quality standards and expertise lenses, not proof of external validation.

### Fatal Flaw — Technique Without Application or Self-Diagnosis
The most common failure mode for OV-SPORT books is presenting technique instruction at the drill or mechanics level without bridging to real-play or field application, and without a fault catalog that allows the reader to self-diagnose when the technique breaks down under pressure. A book that drills fundamentals without situational application and without fault diagnosis produces readers who can perform the skill in isolation but not deploy it when it matters. Every chapter review must confirm that Stages 3 and 4 of the Skill-Progression Architecture are present before the chapter is considered complete.

### KDP Description Strategy

The description leads with the specific reader goal or problem the book solves — not with the method, not with a list of chapters, not with a generic "whether you're a beginner or advanced" opener.

**For sport technique titles:** Open with the most common frustrating failure state the reader is experiencing. Example: "If your drives keep slicing right no matter what you try, the problem is almost certainly in the first three inches of your takeaway — and this book shows you exactly how to fix it with a step-by-step drill sequence."

**For coaching titles:** Open with the specific coaching challenge or athlete development goal. Example: "Coaching youth athletes is not the same as coaching adult players — this session-by-session guide gives you age-appropriate progressions, ready-to-use practice plans, and the developmental framework to build players who love the game."

**For outdoor recreation and hunting/fishing titles:** Open with the experience the reader wants to have, then pivot to the preparation that makes it possible. Example: "Landing your first backcountry trout on a fly rod is the kind of moment that stays with you — this complete technique and preparation guide takes you from your first cast to reading water like an experienced angler."

**For survival titles:** Open with the preparedness motivation, not with fear. Example: "Knowing you can handle an unexpected night out, a navigation failure, or a sudden weather change turns any backcountry trip from an anxious experience into a confident one."

The description body must not contain specific performance-outcome promises that were not supported in the book. It may describe the book's structure (the four-stage skill progression, the session-plan templates, the fault catalog for every technique). It must include a clear statement of what the book is for: its audience, its format, its scope, and its most distinctive feature. For hunting and fishing titles, the description must include: *"Regulatory information in this book reflects sources available at time of writing. Verify current regulations with the applicable wildlife agency before your outing."*

## Key Rules — Do NOT Break

1. **SR2 is structural, not optional.** Every skill or technique family must complete all four stages of the Skill-Progression Architecture: Fundamentals, Drills with Measurable Standards, Game or Field Application, and Self-Diagnosis Fault Catalog. A chapter missing any stage is incomplete and is a Gate 3 HOLD.

2. **Drill standards must be measurable at three levels.** No drill is complete without a beginner standard, intermediate standard, and advanced standard stated as a specific quantity, accuracy rate, or observable outcome. Vague practice instructions are not acceptable.

3. **The PREPARATION SPINE must precede all technique content in outdoor, fishing, hunting, and survival titles.** It cannot be moved to an appendix. It must include all four components in order: gear list (brand-generic), conditions assessment, navigation basics, leave-no-trace ethics.

4. **The FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY is mandatory for survival, bushcraft, and hunting titles.** Life-safety content must be sourced against named recognized outdoor-safety authorities. No survival bravado. The high-stakes disclaimer callout must appear on all life-safety section pages throughout the book, not only at the FIELD-SAFETY BATTERY opening.

5. **All regulatory content in hunting and fishing titles carries the MKT modifier: jurisdiction label, date label, and reader-verification call to action at every section close.** Unverifiable regulatory content is a Gate 4 HOLD. The regulations-currency disclaimer must appear on the copyright page.

6. **OV-HEALTH R2 applies without modification to all training programs.** Any periodized training plan, conditioning schedule, or multi-week workout program in any OV-SPORT book inherits the full contraindication screening, modification table, stop-training warning signs, and referral trigger requirements. A single training week that lacks its modification table is a Gate 3 HOLD.

7. **Gear recommendations are brand-generic throughout.** No commercial brand names in gear lists, equipment sections, or buying guides. Specifications and certification standards may be named; manufacturer names may not.

8. **Youth content meets USOC Safe Sport standards.** No instruction implying unsupervised one-on-one contact, no communication protocols bypassing transparent supervision, athlete autonomy language preserved throughout.

9. **Performance-outcome claims are qualified.** Specific outcome claims in body text must state the assumed starting level, required training commitment, individual variation caveat, and evidence basis. Unqualified specific-outcome promises are prohibited.

10. **The exact user-supplied title is preserved at every stage.** No silent rewrites, translations, shortenings, or optimizations. Recommendations for title changes are surfaced as options only, with user confirmation required before any change is made.

11. **The Coaching Session-Plan Module and Age-Appropriate Progression Tables are mandatory for every skill-family chapter in coaching sub-niche titles.** A coaching chapter without a complete session-plan template is incomplete and is a Gate 3 HOLD.

12. **No fabricated endorsements, governing-body approvals, or tour/association affiliations.** Expert roles are expertise lenses and quality standards. The system does not claim that named external professionals, governing bodies, or official organizations reviewed or endorsed the content.

13. **The PREPARATION SPINE's navigation basics section must include a bailout-route identification requirement.** The reader must be explicitly instructed to identify and pre-record at least one bailout route and turnaround point before any multi-day or remote outing. Omitting this requirement from the navigation section is a Gate 3 HOLD.

14. **The fault catalog in Stage 4 must name faults as observable symptoms, not jargon labels.** "The slice" is acceptable. "Lateral sway causing an out-to-in swing path" requires translation to "You're coming over the top — your club head is moving left-to-right at impact, sending the ball to the right of your target." The self-diagnosis tool must be usable by the reader without an instructor present.

15. **Do not mark the manuscript print-ready until all four gates pass.** A gate check is evidence-based — a checked box means verifiable evidence exists, not merely that the task was attempted.
