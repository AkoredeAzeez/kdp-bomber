# UAPF Niche: Health, Fitness & Wellness (OV-HEALTH)​‍‍​‌‍‌‌‍‍‌‌‍‌‌‌‍‍‍‍‍‌‍‍‍‌‍‌‌‌‌‍‌‌‍‌​

> **Framework integration:** the machine-executable projection of this niche's framework lives in `frameworks/AIRF-2.0-Health-Edition/` (config, validation, phases, and deterministic ops in `genie_health.py`). This SKILL.md remains the authoritative source; that folder never overrides it. At Phase 0 stamp `framework=AIRF 2.0 Health Edition` into the `book_lock.md` classification line (global rule #16).

**Routed here when:** the title or subtitle contains any of: *fitness, workout, exercise, yoga, chair yoga, Pilates, strength training, resistance, weight loss, nutrition, wellness, mindfulness, meditation, breathwork, running, walking, cardio, flexibility, stretching, mobility, balance, fall prevention, tai chi, qi gong, senior fitness, healthy living* — or the format signals an exercise/practice instruction book, a movement program, a mind-body practice guide, or a wellness lifestyle guide. Cross-signals: if the title is primarily a cookbook route to UCGF; if primarily self-help/psychology with no physical practice component, route to OV-SELFHELP; hybrid titles (e.g., "Mindful Eating") stay here when physical/physiological practice instruction dominates.

**Risk regime: R2 — MANDATORY. This niche can hurt readers. Safety language, contraindication screening, and scope-of-practice boundaries are structural requirements, not editorial polish. R2 cannot be waived, downgraded, or "streamlined" for any title in this niche.**

## Expert Panel

1. **Domain Master — Integrative Movement & Wellness Authority** (lead voice): 25+ years across exercise science, mind-body disciplines, and clinical exercise coordination. Owns cross-discipline consistency, safety-protocol uniformity, evidence-hierarchy discipline, and medical-claim responsibility. Final arbiter; on any safety-critical disagreement the panel adopts the most conservative credible position.
2. **Clinical Exercise Physiologist (ACSM-CEP lens)**: contraindication accuracy, PAR-Q+ screening, heart-rate/RPE prescription, medication-interaction awareness (beta-blockers and HR monitoring, blood thinners, insulin, statins), cardiac and chronic-condition red flags, referral thresholds.
3. **Sub-Niche Specialist** (activated to match the title): tai chi/qi gong master, E-RYT 500 + C-IAYT yoga therapist, PMA-certified Pilates educator, NSCA-CSCS strength coach, flexibility/mobility scientist, walking/cardio physiologist, STEADI-trained balance/fall-prevention PT, MBSR-certified meditation and breathwork instructor, or geriatric/adaptive exercise specialist. Owns technique accuracy, correct terminology (pinyin/Sanskrit with translation), and discipline-specific cueing.
4. **Adaptive & Accessibility Specialist**: universal-design modifications (seated/standing/supine/wall-supported/adaptive) for every technique, chronic-condition adaptations, dignity-preserving age-positive language, trauma-informed cueing, large-print and grayscale-legible design advocacy.
5. **Evidence & Claims Auditor**: verifies every therapeutic claim against peer-reviewed sources, enforces the evidence-hierarchy labeling system, strips diagnosis/prescription/cure language, polices scope-of-practice boundaries, and confirms no invented credentials or endorsements.

The panel is an expertise lens, not a claim of human review. Never state or imply that a clinician, attorney, professional body, or lineage holder reviewed the book unless that review actually occurred and is documented.

## Phase 0 — Title Analysis

Execute in order before any TOC work:

1. **Exact-title lock.** Record the title exactly as supplied. Never rewrite, shorten, translate, or "optimize" it. The title determines the manuscript language; system reports stay in English.
2. **Trademark and clearance screen.** Search the target marketplace's official trademark registry, WIPO Global Brand Database where appropriate, exact-match Amazon listings, and exact-match web results. Watch for protected program and brand names common in this niche: *Pilates equipment brand names (Reformer is generic, STOTT/Balanced Body are not), CrossFit, Zumba, SilverSneakers, Wim Hof Method, MBSR institutional branding, Tai Chi for Arthritis (Dr. Paul Lam program), Otago, A Matter of Balance, LSVT BIG, Buteyko.* Classify CLEAR / CAUTION / HIGH RISK / LEGAL REVIEW RECOMMENDED. This is a publishing-risk screen, not legal advice. If risk exists, disclose it — do not silently alter the title.
3. **Sub-niche detection.** Map the title to one (or a deliberate combination) of the ten AIRF sub-categories: (A) Tai Chi & Qi Gong, (B) Yoga & Chair Yoga, (C) Pilates & Core, (D) Stretching & Flexibility, (E) Walking & Low-Impact Cardio, (F) Strength & Resistance, (G) Balance & Fall Prevention, (H) Meditation & Breathwork, (I) Senior Fitness & Adaptive Exercise, (J) Holistic Wellness & Mind-Body Integration. Activate only the specialist protocols the title needs; never force unrelated specialties in.
4. **Audience profiling.** Assign the primary segment: Active Agers 55–80+ (safety-focused, large-print, seated options mandatory), Stress-Management Professionals 30–60 (science-forward, time-efficient), Rehabilitation/Return-to-Movement (contraindication clarity, gradual loading, healthcare-team language), or Fitness Professionals (certification pathways, teaching methodology, scope-of-practice depth). Audience drives typography, tone, and modification density.
5. **Competitor research and page-count decision.** Identify 5–10 current, closely relevant Amazon paperback/hardcover competitors (exclude journals, low-content, outliers). Record title, author, date, format, trim, page count, image density, positioning, review patterns, content gaps. Compute range, arithmetic average, and median. Recommend a target inside the proven competitor range unless a documented gap justifies deviation, adjusted for trim, typography, image plan, tables, trackers, front/back matter.
6. **Subtitle generation.** Only after the trademark screen. Subtitles must be screened for: trademark conflict, weight-loss promises, medical-outcome claims, cure/treatment language, and marketplace compliance. Compliant subtitle patterns: audience + method + benefit-as-capability ("Gentle Chair Yoga Routines to Build Strength, Balance, and Confidence After 60") — never outcome-guarantee patterns ("Lose 20 Pounds," "Heal Your Back," "Reverse Diabetes").
7. **Pen name.** Obtain a random culturally plausible identity (First Name M. Surname format) matched to the manuscript language/market. Screen against prominent niche authors and public figures. Never invent medical, therapeutic, academic, or fitness credentials for the pen name. Identical across title page, copyright page, headers, cover, metadata.
8. **Auto-configuration report (Pre-TOC).** Report: exact title + language, trademark result + databases searched, marketplace + category, competitor table with average/median, recommended page target + justification, recommended subtitle, pen name, body size and spacing with rationale, trim and margins, unique design fingerprint, image plan (images always generated and inserted automatically wherever the content needs them, at any page count), and the exact closing line: **Type Proceed to generate the Table of Contents.** (Per the UAPF proceed gate, auto-type PROCEED via computer use after presenting.)

## Book Architecture

**Structural spine (non-negotiable order):**

1. Front matter: half-title (optional) → title page → copyright page (with health/exercise disclaimer) → optional dedication/acknowledgments → Preface (exactly 1 rendered page) → How to Use This Book (exactly 1 rendered page) → native Word TOC → Introduction (printed page 1, new section).
2. **Chapter 1 is ALWAYS the medical-clearance and safety chapter.** No exercise, pose, breath technique, or program instruction may appear anywhere in the book before this chapter. It contains: PAR-Q+-style readiness screening, when to obtain physician clearance, absolute and relative contraindications for the book's discipline, medication considerations, pain-vs-discomfort education, warning signs and stop-exercise rules, emergency guidance, and how to talk to a healthcare provider about the program. This chapter is referenced (not repeated) by every technique chapter.
3. Foundations block (1–2 chapters): relevant anatomy/physiology in plain language, breath mechanics, equipment and space setup, how progression works.
4. Technique/practice core (the bulk): built from the **Technique Unit** (below), organized foundation → intermediate → advanced or by body region/program week, with logical progression and safety gates between levels.
5. Program chapter(s): structured plans (7-day, 14-day, 30-day, or 12-week as fits the title) assembled only from techniques already taught.
6. Troubleshooting/modification chapter: plateaus, common errors, pain flare protocol, adapting around bad days.
7. Conclusion: approximately 1.5 rendered pages — safe continuation, realistic progress expectations, self-monitoring, when to seek professional support. No mechanical chapter recap.
8. Back matter (title-specific, never boilerplate): readiness/safety checklists, trackers (exercise, walking, balance, sleep, breath, habit), modification ladders, quick-reference technique sheets, questions for a physician/PT/instructor, emergency warning signs, glossary, resources. References (APA 7th default) when evidence claims warrant.

**The Technique Unit** — every exercise, pose, form, or breath practice uses this structure, in this order:

1. Name (traditional term with pinyin/Sanskrit + translation where applicable)
2. Purpose and what it develops
3. **Contraindications and cautions (STRUCTURAL — appears before instructions, every unit, no exceptions):** who should skip or modify this, condition-specific flags (e.g., glaucoma/inversions, osteoporosis/loaded flexion, joint replacement, hypertension/Valsalva, pregnancy, vestibular disorders, hyperventilation risk for breathwork), and the stop rule.
4. Setup: equipment, space, starting position with precise alignment cues
5. Step-by-step execution: exact durations, reps, sets, holds, rest, breathing pattern, RPE or intensity guidance
6. **Universal modifications (MANDATORY every unit): seated version, standing/supported version, adaptive version** (wall, chair, strap, block, band, assistive device), plus regression and progression
7. Common errors and corrections
8. **Step-by-step image sequence (MANDATORY every unit):** NOT a single image of the finished pose. Every technique gets a numbered photo sequence covering the movement phases — minimum 3 panels (starting position → key movement phase(s) → end position/hold); 4–5 panels for multi-phase techniques. Sequence consistency rules: the SAME demonstrator across all panels of one technique (identical person description, clothing, chair/equipment, background, and lighting locked in every panel's prompt); camera angle chosen once per technique for maximum instructional clarity and held constant unless a phase genuinely needs a second angle (then label it "detail view"). Each panel is captioned "Step N: [action]" and carries alt text. Panels are laid out as a side-by-side row (2-up or 3-up via table cells at equal widths) or a vertical progression strip per the design fingerprint. Manifest slots are per-panel: IMG-XX-S1, IMG-XX-S2, etc. A single hero image per chapter opener is additional, not a substitute.

**Page bands:** follow the competitor-derived target. Typical bands: gentle/senior practice guides 120–180 pp; single-discipline instruction 140–220 pp; program-driven fitness 130–200 pp; comprehensive/professional references 220–350 pp. Images are generated and inserted automatically per chapter wherever the content needs them, at ANY page count; page count never removes or defers a needed image.

## Interior Design

- **Trim:** 8.5 × 11 in, 0.7-in margins (default; approved exception only via Pre-TOC report).
- **Typeface:** Times New Roman throughout. Body 11/12/13/14 pt chosen by audience — senior/large-print audiences get 13–14 pt with 1.5 spacing and generous white space; professional references may use 11–12 pt at 1.15.
- **Headings:** Chapter = 16 pt bold BLOCK LETTERS centered, Heading 1. Subchapter = Title Case bold left-aligned, Heading 2. Heading 3 only when genuinely needed.
- **Alignment:** body justified; bullets, numbered steps, checklists, exercise steps, warnings, and questions left-aligned.
- **Safety callouts:** contraindication/warning blocks get a visually distinct, consistent callout treatment (defined in the design fingerprint) that remains legible in grayscale.
- **PREMIUM DESIGN SYSTEM (MANDATORY):** every book in this niche ships with a premium interior, not plain manuscript formatting. Required elements, each executed in the book's fingerprint palette:
  1. *Chapter openers:* full-styled opener — part/chapter label in accent color, chapter title in principal color, a colored rule or motif line under the title, and a chapter hero image or opening callout. Never a bare centered heading on white.
  2. *Technique unit headers:* each exercise opens with a shaded header bar (principal color fill, white or near-white text via table-cell shading) carrying the technique number and name — the reader can flip and find any exercise by its color bar.
  3. *Callout panels:* contraindication/caution blocks in a tinted panel (light accent shade, darker accent border rule) with a bold colored lead-in word (CAUTION / SKIP IF / STOP RULE). Tip and breath cues get a second, visually distinct panel style.
  4. *Colored typographic accents:* subheadings in principal color; step numbers and bullet glyphs in accent color; captions in gray italic; running footer folio styled in principal color.
  5. *Tables:* header rows shaded in principal color with white text; alternating row tints where rows exceed 4.
  6. *White space discipline:* generous spacing around images and panels — premium means uncrowded; never let two callout panels touch.
  All colored elements must remain fully legible when printed grayscale (verify: tint contrast ≥ 25% gray equivalent difference from text). The palette itself comes from the design fingerprint; the *system* above is constant across health titles while its geometry and colors stay unique per book.
- **No-Two-Books-Alike:** before formatting, record a design fingerprint — 2 principal colors + 1–2 accents, chapter opener, divider motif, table header, callout geometry, running header/footer, and image rhythm (full-width hero / half-page instructional / side-by-side sequence / progression strip / inset close-up). Must differ structurally, not just chromatically, from every prior Pegasus title.
- **Word production:** native styles (Title, Subtitle, Heading 1–3, Normal, List Bullet, List Number, Caption, TOC 1–3), genuine TOC field with hyperlinks and dot leaders, section break before Introduction, no folios in front matter, Arabic numbering restarts at 1 on Introduction, captions/bookmarks/cross-reference fields, all fields refreshed before delivery.
- Everything else inherits UAPF default.

## Content Rules

**The fatal flaw to avoid: promising outcomes the reader's body may not deliver — and instructing movement without screening.** A health book that guarantees weight loss, cures, or medical outcomes is both a compliance failure and a reader hazard. A technique presented without its contraindications is a defect equivalent to a missing ingredient in a recipe.

Hard constraints:

1. **No weight-loss promises.** No target pounds, no timelines, no "melt fat," no before/after framing. Weight management may be discussed educationally (energy balance, NEAT, evidence on walking and metabolic health) with individual-variation caveats.
2. **No medical-outcome claims, no diagnosis, no prescription.** Never "heals," "cures," "treats," "reverses," or "replaces your doctor." The book educates; licensed professionals diagnose and prescribe. Referral language wherever risk exceeds book scope.
3. **Evidence hierarchy transparent in the text.** Label the basis of every health-related claim using consistent language: *established evidence* (systematic reviews/clinical guidelines), *preliminary evidence* (early or small studies), *traditional explanation* (e.g., qi, prana, meridians — presented respectfully as the tradition's framework, never as verified physiology), *professional consensus*, and *uncertain/contested*. Never let a traditional explanation borrow scientific authority, and never dismiss it either.
4. **Contraindication screening is structural.** Every Technique Unit carries its contraindication block before the instructions. Generic chapter-top disclaimers do not satisfy this.
5. **Universal modifications mandatory.** Seated, standing/supported, and adaptive options for every technique. Never present a modification as universally safe — state readiness criteria and stop rules.
6. **Exact prescription variables.** Durations, reps, sets, rest, holds, breathing ratios, RPE/heart-rate methods, readiness criteria, stop rules. No vague "do a few reps."
7. **Conservative rule.** When credible sources disagree on a safety-critical point, adopt the most conservative interpretation.
8. **Cultural respect.** Traditional practices (tai chi, yoga, qi gong, pranayama, meditation lineages) get lineage acknowledgment, correct terminology with translation, and non-appropriative framing.
9. **Source standards.** Prefer ACSM, NSCA, WHO, NIH, CDC, AHA, National Institute on Aging, Yoga Alliance, IAYT, PMA, CDC STEADI, and peer-reviewed journals. Safety-critical content: 100% accuracy requirement, cross-checked across multiple authoritative sources.
10. **Anti-AI voice.** Natural practitioner voice; no "Let's dive into," "It's important to note," formulaic conclusions, marketing superlatives, fabricated case studies, or invented endorsements. Honest acknowledgment of evidence gaps.
11. **No filler.** Every section must improve safety, skill, understanding, adherence, problem-solving, or professional value.
12. **Images:** photorealistic, anatomically believable, safe demonstrated form, correct equipment scale, varied demographics/angles/environments, never covering text, always captioned with meaningful alt text. Negative constraints: no distorted limbs, unsafe form, extra fingers, sexualized presentation, or medical misrepresentation. **Every technique carries its full step-by-step sequence (min. 3 panels, same demonstrator throughout the sequence) per the Technique Unit — a single finished-pose image is a Gate 2 failure.** Demographics vary BETWEEN techniques, never within one technique's sequence.

## KDP Positioning

- **Category tree:** Health, Fitness & Dieting → the matching sub-branch (Exercise & Fitness → Yoga / Pilates / Weight Training / Aerobics / Injuries & Rehabilitation; Aging → Exercise & Fitness for seniors titles; Alternative Medicine → Meditation for mindfulness/breathwork; Mental Health for stress-management-leaning titles). Choose the deepest accurate node plus one adjacent discovery node.
- **Description leads with:** who the book is for and what they'll be able to DO (capability language: "build the strength to rise from any chair," "practice a full session without leaving your seat") — never outcome guarantees. Second beat: the safety-first structure (screening chapter, modifications for every exercise) as a differentiator. Third beat: what's inside (technique count, programs, trackers, image support).
- **Metadata signals:** keywords built from sub-niche + audience + modality + qualifier ("chair yoga for seniors over 60," "beginner strength training women," "fall prevention exercises at home"). Never use competitor brand names, protected program names, or medical-claim keywords ("cure," "treatment," "therapy" as a promise) in metadata. Age/audience qualifiers in the subtitle field where compliant.
- Large-print positioning is a legitimate differentiator for senior titles when the 14 pt/1.5 typography decision was made — state it honestly, only if true.

## Key Rules — Do NOT Break

1. **R2 is mandatory and non-waivable.** Every title in this niche runs the full risk regime — no streamlining, no exceptions.
2. **Chapter 1 is the medical-clearance and safety chapter.** No exercise or practice instruction may precede it anywhere in the book.
3. **Contraindications before instructions in every single Technique Unit.** A chapter-top disclaimer never substitutes for per-technique screening.
4. **No weight-loss promises, no medical-outcome claims, no diagnosis, no prescription — anywhere:** manuscript, subtitle, cover, description, keywords, A+ content.
5. **Universal modifications (seated / standing-supported / adaptive) for every technique**, each with readiness criteria and a stop rule; never claim a modification is universally safe.
6. **Evidence hierarchy transparent:** every health claim labeled established / preliminary / traditional / consensus / uncertain; traditional explanations never dressed as verified science, never dismissed either.
7. **Most conservative credible interpretation wins** every safety-critical source conflict.
8. **Never invent credentials, expert review, clinical validation, or endorsements** — for the pen name, the panel, or the marketing.
9. **Exact title preserved; trademark screen before subtitle/TOC;** protected program names (SilverSneakers, Wim Hof, Otago, LSVT BIG, etc.) never used as if generic.
10. **Exact prescription variables in every instruction** — durations, reps, sets, rest, breath, intensity — plus warning signs and stop-exercise rules wherever relevant.
11. **Cultural respect is a quality gate:** correct pinyin/Sanskrit with translation, lineage acknowledgment, no appropriation.
12. **Images show safe, anatomically correct form only** — an image demonstrating unsafe alignment is a Gate 2 hard failure.
13. **No two books alike:** unique design fingerprint recorded and verified against prior Pegasus titles before formatting.
14. **Senior-audience titles get 13–14 pt body text**, 1.5 spacing, high contrast, and grayscale-legible safety callouts.
15. **Every exercise gets a step-by-step image SEQUENCE, never a single pose image.** Minimum 3 panels per technique (start → movement → end), same demonstrator/clothing/setting locked across the sequence, "Step N" captions. One finished-pose image per exercise is a hard Gate 2 failure.
16. **Premium interior is mandatory:** styled chapter openers, shaded technique header bars, tinted safety panels, colored typographic accents, styled tables — executed in the book's unique fingerprint palette and verified grayscale-legible. Plain black-on-white manuscript formatting is a Gate 4 failure in this niche.
