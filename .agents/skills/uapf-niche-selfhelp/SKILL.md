## Book Architecture

### Chapter Formula
Every chapter in an OV-SELF book follows this five-part progression. The components must be executed in this order. No component may be omitted. The depth and length of each component is calibrated to the risk tier and chapter subject.

1. **Relatable Opening Scenario** — Open with a scene, moment, or inner-voice passage that places the reader inside a lived experience connected to the chapter subject. Use second person ("You've just..."; "It's 2 a.m. and..."). This is the "I See You" anchor. It must not be a generic warm-up; it must be specific enough to feel true to the chapter's exact theme. At R4, ensure the opening does not replay trauma graphically — use emotional truth, not clinical detail.

2. **Concept Introduction** — Name the concept, pattern, or skill the chapter will teach. Define it in plain language. Distinguish it from common misconceptions. At R2 and R4, explicitly state what this chapter does NOT do (does not diagnose, does not replace therapy, does not constitute clinical advice).

3. **Evidence-Aware Explanation** — Explain the psychological, behavioral, or neuroscientific reasoning behind the concept, calibrated by tier:
   - R4: Cite peer-reviewed or clinically established sources (APA 7); acknowledge complexity and individual variation; use hedged language ("research suggests," "many people find," "studies have shown")
   - R2: Use professionally supported models; cite credible sources; state evidence limits for contested or emerging frameworks
   - R1: Evidence-aware framing acceptable; may use well-established behavioral models; avoid inflated neuroscience or fabricated statistics
   No statistic, quotation, or research claim may be fabricated. Verify every claim against a real, current source.

4. **Exercises and Reflection** — Provide practical tools calibrated to the risk tier:
   - At R4: All exercises must be optional. Open with explicit permission language: "This exercise is entirely optional. If it feels too much right now, please skip it and return when you feel ready — or not at all." Include a pause prompt. Avoid exercises that require the reader to re-enter traumatic memory without professional support. Exercises may include: gentle journaling prompts, self-compassion phrases, grounding techniques, values clarification, safety mapping.
   - At R2: Exercises encouraged; include a note that readers experiencing significant distress should seek professional support. May include: reflection questions, behavior experiments, communication scripts, boundary-setting practice, thought records.
   - At R1: Exercises are the primary value-delivery mechanism. Use: habit trackers, action plans, goal-setting worksheets, implementation-intention prompts, accountability scripts, decision matrices.

5. **Integration Close** — End each chapter by reconnecting the concept to the reader's larger journey. Reinforce that change is gradual and non-linear. At R4, close with a brief affirmation of the reader's capability and safety. At all tiers, avoid "you've done it!" triumphalism — use encouragement that acknowledges ongoing effort.

### Structural Formula by Risk Tier

**R4 — High-Risk (abuse recovery, eating disorders, anxiety/phobias, grief):**
- Introduction: frame the book as a companion, not a cure; set safety expectations; include first professional-referral notice; introduce the Crisis Resources appendix
- Part I (Chapters 1–3): Understanding the experience — normalize, validate, psychoeducate
- Part II (Chapters 4–7): Building foundations — safety, self-compassion, stabilization, regulation
- Part III (Chapters 8–11): Gentle growth — skills, patterns, integration, identity
- Conclusion (approx. 1.5 pages): Integration, realistic expectations, continuing support, second referral notice
- Appendix A: Crisis Resources (REQUIRED — see Content Rules)
- Appendix B: Exercises Reference (compiled from chapters)
- Appendix C: Recommended Reading / Professional Resources

**R2 — Moderate Therapeutic (relationships, emotional intelligence, mindset, burnout):**
- Introduction: frame the book's scope and limits; professional-referral notice
- Part I (Chapters 1–3): Awareness — understanding patterns, origin, impact
- Part II (Chapters 4–8): Skill-building — tools, frameworks, practice
- Part III (Chapters 9–11): Integration — maintaining gains, relationships, identity
- Conclusion (approx. 1.5 pages): Summary, realistic expectations, continued growth path
- Appendix A: Worksheets and Trackers
- Appendix B: Recommended Reading / Professional Resources

**R1 — Performance (habits, productivity, motivation, success):**
- Introduction: frame the promise and scope; evidence-awareness notice
- Part I (Chapters 1–3): Diagnosis — where you are, what is blocking you
- Part II (Chapters 4–8): System — the core method, components, tools
- Part III (Chapters 9–11): Execution — implementation, accountability, sustainability
- Conclusion (approx. 1.5 pages): Pull it together, long-game framing, encouragement
- Appendix A: Quick-Reference System Summary
- Appendix B: Templates, Trackers, and Worksheets

## Content Rules

### The Fatal Flaw to Avoid
**Therapeutic impersonation with performance packaging** — the most common failure in self-help is writing R4 content (trauma, eating disorders, anxiety) with R1 energy: upbeat language, guaranteed-outcome framing, prescriptive exercises without safety scaffolding, and no referral language. A reader in active grief or eating disorder recovery who receives "5 Steps to Heal in 30 Days" framing may feel shame, may pursue unsupported interventions, or may conclude that professional help is unnecessary. This is the single most dangerous failure mode in self-help publishing. It must be detected and corrected before any chapter is declared complete.

### Hard Constraints (apply at all tiers)
1. **No diagnosis.** The book must not name, imply, or suggest that a reader has a specific clinical diagnosis. Use descriptive, experiential language: "If you've struggled with..." not "If you have borderline personality disorder..."
2. **No treatment claims.** The book does not treat, cure, resolve, or heal any medical or psychiatric condition. It provides educational information, tools, and perspectives. This distinction must appear in the disclaimer and must be maintained in body language throughout.
3. **No guaranteed outcomes.** Do not promise that following the method will produce a specific result within a stated timeframe. Use "may," "can help," "many readers find," "some people experience."
4. **No fabricated research.** Every statistic, study citation, named researcher, and quoted finding must be verifiable against a real, current published source. APA 7 format required.
5. **No fabricated case studies presented as real.** All examples and composite scenarios must either be transparently fictional ("Consider Maria, a composite example...") or sourced from publicly available accounts with appropriate attribution. Never invent a real client or patient.
6. **No invented credentials for the author.** The pseudonymous author carries no degrees, licenses, clinical experience, or professional memberships that have not been established.
7. **No rapid-healing language.** Avoid "heal in X days," "overcome your trauma in a weekend," "finally be free of anxiety," or any framing suggesting quick, total resolution of complex psychological experience.
8. **No coercive exercises.** All exercises must be optional at R4. No exercise may require a reader to confront traumatic memory, disclose private information, or take a high-stakes action without explicit safety scaffolding.
9. **No blame-framing.** Do not imply that the reader's suffering is the result of insufficient effort, wrong thinking, lack of willpower, or inadequate faith. Structural, relational, and biological factors must be acknowledged.
10. **No crisis misinformation.** All crisis hotlines, helpline numbers, professional resource descriptions, and emergency instructions must be verified as current and regionally accurate at time of generation. Never carry forward unverified numbers from a prior session or framework draft.

### R4-Specific Mandatory Content Rules
- **Disclaimer page** must state explicitly that this book is not therapy, does not replace professional mental health care, and is not appropriate as the sole resource for anyone in acute crisis.
- **How to Use This Book** must include: permission to read non-linearly, permission to skip exercises, encouragement to read with a therapist or trusted support person when possible, and a pointer to the Crisis Resources appendix.
- **Every chapter** must include at least one brief professional-referral cue — not a repeated boilerplate paragraph, but a contextually integrated sentence appropriate to the chapter subject.
- **Crisis Resources appendix** is non-negotiable. Must include: national crisis line for the target country, a mental health professional referral pathway, a relevant specialist resource (e.g., eating disorder helpline, domestic violence hotline, grief counseling directory). All resources verified as active at time of generation.
- **Exercises** must open with the optional-exercise declaration and must not require re-entering traumatic memory without professional support.
- **Pacing** — the chapter sequence must not rush the reader. Safety and stabilization chapters come before processing chapters. Do not begin trauma processing in Chapter 1.

### Voice Calibration by Risk Tier
- **R4 voice:** Warm, non-pressuring, validating. Uses "you may," "perhaps," "some readers find," "it is okay if..." Acknowledges that healing is not linear and that reading can itself be a form of self-care. Never uses military metaphor ("fight," "battle," "conquer"). Never uses deficit language ("broken," "damaged," "disordered").
- **R2 voice:** Warm and direct. Can use "you" with slightly more confidence. Acknowledges difficulty while orienting toward growth. Maintains realistic expectations.
- **R1 voice:** Energetic and practical without being aggressive. Warm and encouraging. Honest about effort required. Does not use hyperactive motivational filler or hollow affirmations.

### Anti-AI Language Protocol
The following words and phrases are prohibited as default filler: "leverage," "utilize," "robust," "optimize," "revolutionary," "game-changing," "transformative," "unlock your potential," "dive deep," "it is important to note," "in conclusion" (at any point other than the Conclusion section), "holistic approach," "at the end of the day," "journey" (used more than once per book as a metaphor). These words may appear only when they are genuinely the most precise word in context. Every paragraph must contribute a new idea. Sentence length must vary naturally.

## Key Rules — Do NOT Break

1. **Preserve the exact title.** Never rewrite, correct, translate, or restructure the user's supplied title without explicit user approval.

2. **Assign risk tier before writing any content.** The risk tier (R4, R2, R1) controls safety language, exercises, voice, appendices, and referral requirements. Writing content before the tier is assigned produces uncalibrated and potentially unsafe output.

3. **Never diagnose the reader.** The book must use experiential and descriptive language throughout. No sentence may imply or state that the reader has a clinical diagnosis.

4. **Never make treatment claims.** The phrase "this book will heal," "this method treats," or any functional equivalent is prohibited. The book provides educational information and practical tools.

5. **Never promise guaranteed or timed outcomes.** "Heal in 30 days," "finally overcome," "permanently eliminate" are prohibited. Use "may," "can help," "many readers find," "over time."

6. **R4 exercises must be optional.** Every exercise in an R4 book must open with an explicit, non-shaming optional-exercise declaration. No exceptions, including exercises that seem mild.

7. **Crisis Resources appendix is mandatory at R4.** It must contain verified, active, jurisdiction-appropriate crisis lines and professional resource pathways. It must be present before the manuscript is declared complete. Unverified or outdated contacts are a production blocker.

8. **Never fabricate research.** Every statistic, citation, and named study must be verifiable against a real, current published source. If a claim cannot be verified, reframe it or remove it.

9. **Chapter formula is non-negotiable.** Every chapter must follow: relatable scenario → concept → evidence-aware explanation → exercises/reflection → integration. Skipping the opening scenario, omitting exercises, or ending without integration is a Gate 2 failure.

10. **No two books share the same architecture.** Chapter sequence, opening patterns, exercise types, example set, image arrangement, and appendix package must be designed fresh for each title. Changing names in a prior template is not a new book.

11. **Visual safety is mandatory.** No chapter-opening image, concept illustration, or exercise visual may depict graphic distress, body harm, before/after body transformation, coercive relationships, or realistic weapons. At R4, all images must favor grounded, supported, connected, and hopeful visual concepts.

12. **Use exact "Type Proceed" continuation phrases.** After each phase, the exact phrase specified in the UAPF workflow must appear. "May I proceed?" or informal variations are not acceptable.

13. **Verify crisis information live.** Crisis hotlines, professional body directories, and emergency numbers change. Do not carry forward contact information from any previous session, framework draft, or static document. Verify every resource at the time of generation.

14. **Never attach credentials to the pseudonymous author.** No degrees, licenses, clinical experience, religious office, or professional affiliations may be invented. If the book's marketing requires an author bio, it may reference lived-experience perspective or professional interests only when those are explicitly established by the user.

15. **Apply the anti-AI language protocol.** Prohibited filler terms — leverage, utilize, robust, optimize, revolutionary, game-changing, transformative, unlock, dive deep — may not appear as defaults. Every paragraph must advance a new idea. Voice must feel human, specific, and calibrated to the assigned risk tier.
