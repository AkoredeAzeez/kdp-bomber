## Expert Panel

1. **Domain Master — Pediatric Medicine / Developmental Science**: Applies the medical and developmental accuracy lens. Anchors all milestone information to current AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) or NHS (National Health Service) guidelines as appropriate to the target marketplace. This is a review lens; actual qualified review must be separately engaged before publication.
2. **Perinatal and Maternal Health Specialist**: Reviews pregnancy, birth, and postpartum content for clinical accuracy, safe framing of complications, and appropriate escalation guidance. Verifies that US and UK guidance are not cross-transplanted (AAP ≠ NHS on key protocols).
3. **Child and Adolescent Psychologist**: Applies developmental psychology to milestone ranges, behavioral guidance, discipline strategies, and emotional coaching content. Flags any claims about "normal" development that ignore the range of neurodiversity.
4. **Reassurance-with-Honesty Editor**: Holds the voice standard. This reader is anxious. Every chapter must balance truthful information (including when things go wrong) with non-alarmist framing. Flags catastrophizing language and false reassurance equally.
5. **Family Systems and Relationship Therapist**: Applies to relationship, marriage, divorce, blended family, and co-parenting chapters. Ensures no legal advice is implied, no side is taken in adversarial co-parenting scenarios, and professional referral language is present where required.

## Phase 0 — Title Analysis

**Step 0-A: Sub-Genre Classification (determines architecture)**

Classify into one primary sub-genre:
- **Pregnancy / Prenatal** (trimester-based; covers conception through birth)
- **Newborn & Infant Care** (birth through 12 months; month-by-month architecture)
- **Toddler and Early Childhood** (ages 1–5; developmental-stage chapters)
- **Middle Childhood** (ages 6–12; school-years focus)
- **Teen Parenting / Adolescence** (ages 13–18; peer pressure, identity, digital life)
- **Baby Names Book** (name-entry format; separate architecture)
- **Relationships / Dating / Marriage** (adult relationship focus; not child-development primary)
- **Divorce / Co-Parenting / Blended Family** (post-separation family systems)
- **Grandparenting** (grandparent role, multigenerational)
- **General Parenting Method** (a philosophy or approach spanning multiple age bands)

Record the sub-genre as **Active Sub-Genre**. It controls chapter architecture, expert panel weighting, and content rules.

**Step 0-B: Marketplace and Authority Source Declaration**

Immediately identify the target marketplace and lock the medical authority:
- **US market** → cite AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) guidelines; reference HealthyChildren.org, CDC developmental milestones
- **UK market** → cite NHS guidelines; reference NHS Start for Life, NICE guidelines
- **Canadian market** → cite CPS (Canadian Paediatric Society)
- **Australian market** → cite RACP (Royal Australasian College of Physicians), Raising Children Network
- **Dual-market (US/UK)** → clearly label which guideline applies to each claim; never blend or treat them as interchangeable

Record as **Active Medical Authority**. Cross-contaminating US and UK guidance — e.g., applying US safe-sleep guidelines in a UK book or vice versa — is a factual error that can harm readers. This is a hard prohibition.

**Step 0-C: Title Clearance**

Perform the standard AIRF 2.0 trademark screen with these additional parenting-specific searches:
- Check major parenting series (e.g., "What to Expect," "The Baby Whisperer," "No-Drama Discipline," "The Whole-Brain Child") for title overlap.
- Search major parenting publishers (Workman, Penguin Random House parenting imprints, HarperCollins, Chronicle Books) for similar naming patterns.
- For baby name books: verify the proposed title does not duplicate established name-book franchises (e.g., "The Baby Name Wizard").

**Step 0-D: Age-Range Extraction**

If the title or format signals a specific age band, extract and lock it. Example: "Raising a 3-Year-Old" → Active Age Band = 3 years. The book must not drift significantly outside this band without explicit section labeling.

**Step 0-E: Auto-Configuration**

After sub-genre classification and marketplace identification, auto-configure:
- **Reference style**: APA 7 (default for parenting, developmental science, relationships per AIRF 2.0)
- **Risk level**: R2 baseline for all health-adjacent content (pregnancy, safe-sleep, feeding, developmental screening). Elevate to R3 for postpartum depression, domestic violence, abuse, divorce trauma. Elevate to R4 for pregnancy loss, stillbirth, NICU, or grief content.
- **Voice register**: Reassurance-with-honesty (see Content Rules). Warm, direct, realistic — never catastrophizing or falsely cheerful.
- **"When to Call the Professional" boxes**: mandatory structural element in every health-adjacent chapter (see Content Rules)
- **Disclaimer**: Parenting/medical-adjacent disclaimer required (see Content Rules)

Output a Phase 0 Summary confirming the Active Sub-Genre, Active Medical Authority, age band, reference style, risk level, and voice register before proceeding to competitor research.

## Book Architecture

### Stage-Based Chapter Architecture (Health-Adjacent Sub-Genres)

For **Pregnancy / Prenatal** books:
- Organize by trimester (3 main parts) with month-level sub-chapters where detail warrants
- Part 1: First Trimester (Weeks 1–13) — conception, early symptoms, first appointments, miscarriage acknowledgment
- Part 2: Second Trimester (Weeks 14–27) — growth, anatomy scan, movement, preparing
- Part 3: Third Trimester (Weeks 28–40+) — birth prep, hospital planning, late complications, labor onset
- Plus: Postpartum chapter (weeks 1–6 minimum); Appendices with birth plan template, hospital bag checklist, newborn checklist

For **Newborn & Infant Care** books:
- Organize by month bands: Month 1, Months 2–3, Months 4–6, Months 7–9, Months 10–12
- Each month chapter: What to Expect (development) → Feeding → Sleep → Common Concerns → When to Call the Professional box → Parent Wellbeing note
- Do not compress multiple months into a single paragraph without clear month labeling

For **Toddler / Early Childhood** books:
- Organize by age band: Year 1–2, Year 2–3, Year 3–4, Year 4–5 (or similar based on title scope)
- Each age-band chapter: Developmental Overview → Language → Motor Skills → Social-Emotional → Common Challenges → When to Call the Professional box

For **Teen Parenting** books:
- Organize by developmental theme (identity, peer relationships, digital life, school, risk behavior, mental health) rather than strict age
- Include an explicit chapter on recognizing when professional mental health support is needed

For **General Parenting Method** books:
- Philosophy introduction (what this method is and what it is not) → 6–12 chapters on application domains (discipline, sleep, feeding, big emotions, siblings, school, etc.) → Troubleshooting chapter → When this method needs professional backup

### Baby Names Book Architecture (Separate)

Baby name books use an entry-format structure, not narrative chapters:

**Each name entry must follow this sequence:**
1. **Name** (bold heading)
2. **Origin** (language and culture of origin — no invented origins)
3. **Meaning** (etymological meaning — no speculation or creative reinterpretation; if meaning is contested or uncertain, state that clearly)
4. **Pronunciation** (phonetic guide)
5. **Variants / Alternate Spellings** (list of legitimate documented variants; no invented variants presented as traditional)
6. **Notable Associations** (optional: historical figures, literary characters, cultural references — factual only, no fabricated associations)
7. **Sibling Name Suggestions** (optional: 3–5 names that pair well stylistically)

Organize entries alphabetically or by theme (nature names, classic names, virtue names, etc.). Do not invent etymologies. If an etymology is disputed or unknown, state: "Origin uncertain" or "Multiple theories exist." This is a factual constraint, not a stylistic preference.

### "When to Call the Professional" Box (STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENT)

This box is a mandatory structural element in every health-adjacent chapter — not an optional sidebar. It is not optional, decorative, or subject to removal for space. Format:

```
WHEN TO CALL THE PROFESSIONAL
[Bulleted list of specific signs, symptoms, or situations that warrant immediate medical consultation, a pediatrician call, or emergency services. Calibrated to the chapter topic. Written in plain, non-alarmist language. Jurisdictionally appropriate to the Active Medical Authority.]
```

The box must:
- Appear in every chapter covering physical health, development, feeding, sleep, mental health, or safety
- List at least 3 and no more than 8 specific indicators (not vague generalities like "if you are worried")
- Specify the level of urgency: call your pediatrician, seek urgent care, call emergency services
- Use the Active Medical Authority's language and thresholds (AAP vs. NHS vs. other)
- Be formatted as a Word text box or shaded block with a clear label

## Content Rules

### Medical Authority and Evidence Rules (R2 Battery)

All medical, developmental, and safety information must meet these standards:

- **Safe-sleep guidance**: cite the Active Medical Authority's current recommendation only. Do not blend AAP (back to sleep, firm surface, no bed-sharing recommendation) with NHS (more permissive bed-sharing guidance in some contexts). These are different guidelines; mixing them can cause harm.
- **Feeding guidance**: specify whether the recommendation is for breastfeeding, formula feeding, or both. Cite AAP or NHS as appropriate. Do not present one as superior in ways that shame parents who cannot or choose not to follow it.
- **Developmental milestones**: present ALL milestone information as RANGES, not single ages. Use the format: "Most children [milestone] between [age range]. The typical average is around [average age]. If your child has not [milestone] by [upper age limit], speak with your pediatrician." Never state "By age X, your child WILL..." as this pathologizes typical variation.
- **Immunization schedules**: do not reproduce a specific schedule in full — schedules change, and a static schedule in a book becomes outdated. Instead, direct readers to the CDC (US), NHS (UK), or equivalent current schedule resource and their healthcare provider.
- **Milestone variation and neurodiversity**: acknowledge that developmental ranges include children with autism, ADHD, sensory processing differences, and other developmental profiles. Avoid framing typical neurotypical development as the only valid pathway.
- **Postpartum mental health**: postpartum depression, anxiety, and psychosis are medical conditions. Use clinical language ("postpartum depression," not "baby blues" for clinical-level symptoms). Include the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale reference and a clear instruction to seek professional help. Never suggest that positive thinking, diet, or devotional practice alone treats postpartum depression.
- **Every statistic and study cited must be verified live** before writing. Do not use statistics from memory or from framework documents. Record the source, date accessed, and URL where applicable.

### Milestone Range Presentation (Mandatory Format)

Milestone information must NEVER appear as a single fixed age. Correct format:

> "Most babies take their first independent steps somewhere between 9 and 15 months. The average is around 12 months, though walking at 9 months is just as typical as walking at 15 months. If your child is not walking by 18 months, bring it up with your pediatrician — this is the threshold for a developmental check."

Incorrect format (PROHIBITED):
> "By 12 months, your baby will be walking."

### Reassurance-with-Honesty Voice

This reader is likely anxious, sleep-deprived, or under significant stress. The voice must:
- Acknowledge the difficulty of the reader's stage honestly ("The newborn weeks are genuinely exhausting")
- Deliver reassurance that is grounded and specific, not falsely cheerful ("Most parents feel exactly this way — and it does get more predictable as your baby's sleep patterns develop")
- Name the hard things without catastrophizing ("Postpartum depression affects up to 1 in 5 new mothers. If you are feeling more than ordinary tiredness or sadness, you deserve support")
- Avoid both: (a) relentless cheerfulness that makes struggling parents feel more isolated; and (b) alarmist language that increases anxiety without adding actionable guidance
- Normalize variation: "There is no single right way to do this" and "every family is different" must be more than filler — they must reflect genuine content that validates diverse approaches

### Baby Name Etymology Rules

For baby names books specifically:
- Every origin claim must be traceable to a recognized etymological source (Oxford Dictionary of First Names, Hanks and Hodges, Behind the Name database, or equivalent academic reference)
- If an etymology is uncertain, contested, or folk-etymology (not scholarly), state this explicitly: "The origin of this name is debated; one common explanation is..., though this is not definitively established."
- Do not invent meanings, fabricate cultural connections, or present marketing-friendly meanings as facts ("This name means 'champion of light' in ancient Celtic" — if not sourced, do not write it)
- Do not present spelling variants invented for phonetic reasons as legitimate traditional variants without noting their modern origin

### Relationship, Marriage, and Divorce Rules

- Do not take sides in contested divorce or co-parenting scenarios
- Do not provide legal advice about custody, divorce proceedings, child support, or property division — direct readers to a family law attorney
- When covering domestic violence, include safety resources (current, verified, jurisdiction-appropriate) and do not frame leaving as a simple decision
- Relationship books must not promise that following the book's advice will save or fix a marriage
- Distinguish between relationship difficulties (within scope) and clinical conditions (borderline personality, narcissistic personality disorder, etc.) — do not diagnose partners or encourage readers to diagnose; direct to professional evaluation

### The Fatal Flaw to Avoid

**Fixed-age milestone statements presented without range.** Stating that a child "will" do something at a specific age as fact — rather than as the middle of a wide typical range — generates parental anxiety when children develop at their own pace, can cause parents to miss genuine developmental concerns because they were watching the wrong goalposts, and misrepresents developmental science. Every milestone statement must reflect the actual documented range.

## Key Rules — Do NOT Break

1. **Active Medical Authority must be declared in Phase 0 and held throughout the entire book.** US and UK guidelines are not interchangeable. Cross-contamination is a reader-safety risk, not an editorial style choice.
2. **Every developmental milestone must be presented as a range with an upper threshold for professional consultation.** A single fixed age presented as fact is prohibited.
3. **"When to Call the Professional" boxes are a structural requirement**, not optional. Every health-adjacent chapter must contain one.
4. **Safe-sleep guidance must reflect the current Active Medical Authority recommendation** at the time of generation. Do not carry over older guidelines from previous projects. Verify live.
5. **Immunization schedules must not be reproduced in full in the book body.** Direct readers to the live, current resource for the target marketplace.
6. **Baby name etymologies must be sourced or labeled as uncertain.** Invented etymologies are factual errors, not creative decisions.
7. **The reassurance-with-honesty voice is mandatory.** False cheerfulness and unnecessary alarm are both failures of this standard.
8. **Neurodiversity must be acknowledged** wherever developmental milestones, behavior, or learning are discussed. Neurotypical development is not the only valid pathway.
9. **Postpartum depression and anxiety are medical conditions** requiring clinical referral, not lifestyle advice. Professional help language is mandatory in any postpartum chapter.
10. **No legal advice about divorce, custody, or family law.** Direct readers to qualified family law attorneys for these matters.
11. **Crisis and support resources must be verified live** for current contact information and regional suitability before finalization. Never carry static lists from other projects.
12. **No invented credentials for the pseudonymous author.** No fabricated pediatric experience, certifications, or family history claims.
