# Amazon Ads for Beginners​‍‍​‌‍‌‌‍‍‌‌‍‌‌‌‍‍‍‍‍‌‍‍‍‌‍‌‌‌‌‍‌‌‍‌​
### The short, honest guide — read this before you spend a dollar

Amazon Ads can grow a book, and it is also the fastest way a new author loses
money. This guide gives you the few things that actually matter, in plain
words. Genie handles the math and the weekly decisions; you keep control of the
money. You never hand Genie your password or card, and nothing spends without
your yes.

---

## The one number that runs everything: ACOS

ACOS means "Advertising Cost of Sales." It is simply:

> **money you spent on ads ÷ money those ads made you**

If you spend $10 on ads and they sell $40 of books, your ACOS is 25%.

Now the number that tells you whether that is good or bad: your **break-even
ACOS**. That is your royalty divided by your price. A $14.99 book that pays you
$4.23 has a break-even ACOS of about **28%**.

- ACOS **below** break-even → the ads made you money.
- ACOS **above** break-even → the ads cost you money (which is sometimes fine at
  launch — see below).

Genie computes your exact break-even from your real royalty. You never guess it.

---

## Why your first ads SHOULD lose money (on purpose)

This trips up every beginner. In the first month, you run ads *above* break-even
on purpose. You are not buying profit yet — you are buying **sales velocity,
early reviews, and organic search rank.** A book that climbs the rankings then
sells on its own, without ads. So:

- **Launch (first 30 days):** aim high, up to about 1.5 to 2 times break-even.
  You are investing in rank.
- **Transition (days 30 to 60):** aim to roughly break even. Ads pay for
  themselves while your rank grows.
- **Profit (day 60+):** aim below break-even. Now ads add margin.

Genie tells you which phase you are in and the target for each.

## The one report that matters

Everything Genie decides comes from one file you download: the **search-term
report** from your ads console. It lists every phrase shoppers actually typed,
how many clicks it got, what it cost, and what it sold.

Each week you do one thing: **export that report and drop it to Genie.** Genie
reads it and hands you back an exact action list:
- **Harvest** the phrases that are selling (move them to your best campaign).
- **Negate** the phrases wasting money (more than 10 clicks and zero sales).
- **Bid up** the profitable ones, **bid down** the expensive ones.

You approve anything that spends money; Genie applies the safe, money-saving
changes and logs everything.

---

## The three mistakes that bankrupt new authors

1. **Checking too often and panicking.** Ad data takes days to settle (a click
   today may become a sale next week). Never judge on fewer than 7 days. Genie
   waits for enough data before recommending anything.
2. **Letting losers run.** A phrase with 20 clicks and no sale is not "warming
   up." It is a leak. The weekly negate list plugs it.
3. **Judging by profit in week one.** Launch ads are supposed to run at a loss.
   Judge them by rank and reviews at first, by ACOS later. Quitting in week one
   because "ads lost money" throws away the rank you were paying to build.

## What Genie does and does not do

**Genie does:** compute your break-even and budget plan, read your weekly report,
produce the exact harvest/negate/bid list, apply the money-saving changes, keep
an auditable log, and track whether your organic rank holds when ads pause.

**Genie never:** enter your Amazon password or payment details, launch a
campaign or raise a budget without your explicit yes (with the exact daily
figure stated), or invent a single number. Every figure comes from your real
report.

## Getting started

Tell Genie: **"set up ads for [your book]."** It will compute your break-even,
propose a starting budget for the money you want to risk, and lay out the four
starter campaigns. You confirm each daily budget, log in to your own ads
account, and Genie builds them. Then once a week: export the report, drop it in,
approve the list. That is the whole job.

*Amazon Ads is a MAX-pack capability. Results vary; ads amplify a good listing,
they do not rescue a weak cover, price, or description.*
